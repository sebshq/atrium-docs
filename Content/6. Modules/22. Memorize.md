Title: Memorize

# Memorize | Learn it *by heart.*

<video src="/_assets/guide/memorize-learn.mp4" poster="/_assets/guide/memorize-learn.jpg"></video>

The Memorize card helps you learn passages word for word, such as verses, poems or lines from a speech. You learn a passage in stages, then review it on a schedule that spaces each review out as you get to know it. Everything it keeps is written into the passage's own note.

## Passages | From *your notes.*

There are two ways to make a passage:

- **A whole note.** Put it in the card's **Folder**, or add `memorize` to its tags in the properties.
- **A paragraph or list item anywhere.** Tag it `#memorize`, such as `Be still, and know that I am God. #memorize`.

Headings, links and formatting are left out of what you learn.

## Learning | Read, *then recall.*

A session goes through four stages. Each stage is answered word by word:

1. **Read**: the whole passage is shown.
2. **Half hidden**: every other word is cut to its first letter.
3. **All hidden**: every word is cut to its first letter.
4. **Test**: every word is blank.

A run of 90% or better opens the next stage straight away. A run below 90% shows the words you missed in red and starts the stage again. If you stop there, the passage comes back in ten minutes. The card keeps the stage each passage has reached, so you pick up where you left off.

## Answering | Three *ways.*

The icons under the passage switch how you answer, at any point, keeping the words already done:

- **First letters**: type each word's first letter, and the word is written out.
- **Whole words**: type each word, then <kbd>Space</kbd>. A one-letter slip still counts.
- **Spoken** (macOS 26 or later): say the passage aloud, and each word lights up as it's heard. **Listening** shows the microphone is on; click it to pause. Your voice is transcribed on your Mac and never leaves it.

The card starts with first letters for a new passage, then moves to spoken and whole words as you know it better.

## Reviews | Spaced *to fit.*

Passing the Test schedules a passage's next review with [FSRS](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki/wiki/ABC-of-FSRS), the scheduler Anki uses. It sets the next review from how well you did. The next review comes in a few days, then in weeks, then in months. A review below 70% sends the passage back to All hidden, to learn again in ten minutes.

The card lists what's due first, then new passages, up to **New passages per day** (5 unless you change it), then the rest by date.

## Long passages | *Piece by piece.*

A note over 60 words is learned in pieces of about 40 words, in order. The next piece opens once you've learned the one before it. A heading or a horizontal rule (`---`) always starts a new piece, and the card names each piece after the heading above it, such as *Luke 1 · 3 of 35 · Birth of John the Baptist Foretold*.

Each piece after the first opens with the last sentence before it, faint, to lead you in. The up chevron above it shows earlier pieces, one more each click.

## Your notes | What it *writes.*

A note keeps its review in properties, `memorize-due`, `memorize-stability` and the like. A long note keeps one line per piece in `memorize-parts`. A tagged paragraph keeps its review in a field at its end, `[memorize:: 2026-11-18 …]`. Each save can be undone with <kbd>⌘Z</kbd>.

## Keys | *On the list.*

With the card picked out, <kbd>↩</kbd> goes to the first passage and <kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>↓</kbd> move between them. <kbd>↩</kbd> starts a session and <kbd>o</kbd> opens the passage's note. In a session, <kbd>Esc</kbd> stops. A passage's right-click menu adds **Skip until tomorrow** and **Reset progress**.
